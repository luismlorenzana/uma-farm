The story pack: a menu

For the story session. This page lists everything the game can read from a story pack: what each part does in play, and what plays when it's missing. It's a menu, not a checklist:

  • every part is optional;
  • any number of characters is fine, in any order, including none;
  • nothing is counted: no fixed opening, list sizes, rungs or places;
  • a partial pack is the normal case, and any pack can be released.

Write only what you want the game to show. Everything else in content/story/ is yours: the game never reads it. Whatever you leave out, the game plays its default story: a complete story every Uma plays from day one (talks by stage and temperament, gift reactions, every event from meeting her to married life, the wedding night, her occasions, the evenings' scenes, morning greetings and meals), written once for the game with her name, her favourite good and her line's work filled in. It plays whole and pays as written ones do, so nothing waits on the story (the full list is in what plays without the story). Your writing replaces it piece by piece: her chat set for a stage replaces the default talks for it, an event of an id replaces that event, her gifts replace her default tastes, and anything you haven't written stays the default, so ship whatever's written. The Farm Story Sandbox marks, for each character, what's written and what's still the default. How your work reaches the game is in F40; how the game plays is in the game mechanics. The page view is story-pack.html.

Errors and notes

The game's reader, shared by the game, the release and the Farm Story Sandbox, finds two kinds of things.

The words

The owner's standing direction for anything on screen (the placeholder and school are notes):

  • {farmer} is the one placeholder: the farmer's name, filled as the line shows. Any other {…} shows as written.
  • The protagonist is a man (the owner, 2026-11-14): named by the player, never drawn. A line may say he, her husband, the groom; no word about him is a note any more.
  • Nothing reads as school.

Sisters

A sister is an outfit of an Uma, presented as a character of her own, with her own name and profile: the Uma Ranker's, named s<outfitId> (s and her outfit's id). The pack takes her id wherever it takes a character's: the cast, the retired welcome list, a tie's a or b, her bible umas/s<outfitId>.json, her route routes/s<outfitId>.json and a pair's file.

  • Her face, bust, talents and temperament are her outfit's, from master data: an outfitId on her entry is ignored (a note).
  • Her name is her entry's name, from the pack's private release at run time, never git. Without one, she shows as her elder's sister (a note).
  • She lives in town and is out there like everyone else, with her own home, job and routine if you give her them, and is met like anyone: by introducing yourself in town.

Her life in town

Every Uma is a retired racer with a life in town: a home, a part-time job, the places she likes. Three optional fields on her cast entry say where she is, when, and the town map lists who's at each place now (who's where). Days are turns and nothing reads a clock, so "when" is days of the season and seasons:

  • A location is one of the town map's: the six spots (plaza, market, cafe, harbour, library, track), the four shops and offices (store, rancher, mayor, smith), or one town.json adds (below). One the map doesn't hold is an error that skips the field (or the stop).

  • Her goals and objectives live in her bible, as before.

Her chapters

Her story is hers, in her own chapters, from meeting her to married life. A chapter groups her events along the game's backbone: friendship F1–F10 and the confession, romance R1–R10, the proposal and the wedding, then married life. The festivals

Each festival is { "id", "name", "season", "day", "happens" }, its day a season and a day of it, 1–12.

  • One of the game's five, by its id (flower-show, fishing-derby, cooking-contest, animal-show, harvest-cup): renamed by its name, moved by its season and day (each defaulting to the game's), and happens says what happens. It keeps its game rule under the story's name, wherever it falls: its festival night, the morning's news, a festival trigger or gate naming its id, and the contest its rank challenge counts (the Flower Show's, the Fishing Derby's, the Cooking Contest's, the Animal Show's) when the festivals are built. A moved one plays all of it on its new day, every year, and none on its old one. The game's five are never removed.
  • Any other id is one of the story's own, added: it needs its name, season and day. It's a festival day: the calendar marks it, the morning says it, festival night opens, and a festival trigger or gate may name its id. It has no contest.
  • One festival a day. The game's five take their days first (where the story puts them); a move onto a day another holds is skipped, and that festival keeps the game's day. Then the story's own, in the file's order: one on a day already held is skipped.
  • The calendar lists each with its happens under it.

Shipping

pnpm story:ship --note "what's new, in one line" puts the pack on the owner's phone. Run it at the end of every writing session, on the machine where the story is written (macOS or Windows). In order:

StepWhat happens
1. CheckReads content/story/ with the game's own reader and prints what it skips (the errors) and the notes. Nothing blocks
2. ReleaseBuilds the release into the ignored content/story-release/: the pack as the game reads it, every broken part already skipped, every other field kept
3. PublishNumbers the release (one more than the bucket's current one, or 1), dates it and gives it its note, then uploads the new hash-named files first and story.json last to the farm's private bucket
4. Back uppnpm story:push farm in the sibling ../uma-platform checkout, when it's there; skipped with a note when it isn't
5. SayThe release's number, its date and its note
  • The note is --note "…", else the first line of ship.md in the pack's folder, else none. It's one line of at most 160 characters, since the morning shows it; a longer one stops the ship before anything is uploaded.
  • On the phone: the next morning on an existing farm opens New today with "New story arrived · your note", once a release, and Account shows Story: Release n, date. A new farm meets the story in its opening.
  • Nothing new, nothing published: a pack the bucket already plays keeps its number, and the drafts are still backed up. A ship that stopped partway is safe to run again.
  • --dry-run reads the bucket (the number, which files are there) and lists what it would upload, uploading nothing.
  • Rolling back: pnpm story:ship --release <n> publishes release n's index again, numbered anew, with the note "Rolled back to n". The bucket keeps every file and the last ten indexes.
  • The machine: gcloud is called with the environment only, never changing the account or its configuration. STORY_GCLOUD_CONFIG names the configuration (default default), STORY_GCLOUD_PROJECT the project (uma-rpg), STORY_BUCKET the bucket (uma-farm-content) and STORY_PLATFORM_DIR the uma-platform checkout (../uma-platform).
  • The steps alone: pnpm story:release builds the release (any pack releases; it refuses only when there's no pack folder), and pnpm story:publish uploads it, unnumbered, so the morning doesn't announce it. The ship is the routine.

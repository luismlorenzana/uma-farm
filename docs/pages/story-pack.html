The story pack: a menu

For the story session. This page is the story's standing guide: first how the story reaches the game, then everything the game can read from a story pack, what each part does in play, and what plays when it's missing. It's a menu, not a checklist:

  • every part is optional;
  • any number of characters is fine, in any order, including none;
  • nothing is counted: no fixed opening, list sizes, rungs or places;
  • a partial pack is the normal case, and any pack can be released.

Write only what you want the game to show. Everything else in content/story/ is yours: the game never reads it. Whatever you leave out, the game plays its default story: a complete story every Uma plays from day one (talks by stage and temperament, gift reactions, every event from meeting her to married life, the wedding night, her occasions, the evenings' scenes, morning greetings and meals), written once for the game with her name, her favourite good and her line's work filled in. It plays whole and pays as written ones do, so nothing waits on the story (the full list is in what plays without the story). Your writing replaces it piece by piece: her chat set for a stage replaces the default talks for it, an event of an id replaces that event, her gifts replace her default tastes, and anything you haven't written stays the default, so ship whatever's written. The Farm Story Sandbox marks, for each character, what's written and what's still the default. How your work reaches the game is the first part, below; how the game plays is in the game mechanics. The page view is story-pack.html.

How the story reaches the game

The story session is free. It builds the world, the backgrounds and the characters however it wants, and much of it may never appear in the game: it informs the dialogue that does. There are no layers, waves, counts or required shapes. This part says how the story's work reaches the game; the rest of the page is the menu of what the game reads.

What's the story's

  • Everything about the story: world building, backgrounds and histories; characters and their voices, relationships, routes and scenes. Any form, any length, any order, and as many characters as the story wants.
  • Who: any character in master data, by her characterId, and the Uma Ranker's sisters (sisters).
  • Her life: her chapters, her home, her job and her routine, her scenes and her words are the story's choice, every one optional (the cast).

Where it lives

content/story/, on the machine where the story is written, which git ignores. Organise it as you like: the game reads only the pack's files (the files), and anything else there is yours.

Reading it: the Farm Story Sandbox

The Farm Story Sandbox is the story's reading tool, served on the machine where the story is written (pnpm stories, http://127.0.0.1:4321). It reads the pack with the game's own reader:

  • each character: her story piece by piece, hers, the default and missing, as pnpm story:status shows it (a complete Uma); what's written and what still plays the default story, her chapters, her route and her bible;
  • the World page: what the game will use of world.json, beside the world bible;
  • the pack's errors and notes (errors and notes);
  • Review panels: the owner reads there, and each panel writes the owner's verdict to content/story/reviews.json.

Shipping it

The routine, at the end of every writing session, in this repo's checkout where the story is written (macOS or Windows; Node 22.18 or later):

StepCommandWhat it says when it worked
1. Statuspnpm story:status --tier 1Each Uma's pieces, hers · default · missing; "10 of 10 complete" once Tier 1 is whole
2. Shippnpm story:ship --note "what's new, in one line"Its last line: "Release n shipped, date: note"
3. Confirmpnpm story:ship --dry-run"The bucket already plays this pack as release n: nothing to publish"
4. SayTell the owner "Release n is out"On their phone, Account shows Story: Release n, date, and the next morning opens with "New story arrived"
  • The ship is the publish. The live game's API reads the bucket's story.json on every request, so a release is live the moment step 2 ends: no deploy, no commit and no pull request. The story never goes into git.
  • "Nothing new shipped" means the bucket already plays this pack: there's nothing to publish.
  • Ship partial work freely: a pack only ever adds to what the game already plays. The owner can ask for a ship at any time ("ship it").
  • Ship from one machine, the one where the story is written. A ship publishes the folder it reads, so a ship from an older copy publishes the older pack as a new release and backs its drafts up over the newer ones. On any other machine, bring the shipped drafts first: pnpm story:pull farm --drafts --force in ../uma-platform, which replaces this machine's copy with the last ship's. The tell: on a machine that's current, step 3 says "already plays" before anything's written there.

What the ship does, step by step:

StepWhat happens
1. CheckReads content/story/ with the game's own reader and prints what it skips (the errors) and the notes, then each Uma with a route as pnpm story:status shows her (a complete Uma). Nothing blocks
2. ReleaseBuilds the release into the ignored content/story-release/: the pack as the game reads it, every broken part already skipped, every other field kept
3. PublishNumbers the release (one more than the bucket's current one, or 1), dates it and gives it its note, then uploads the new hash-named files first and story.json last to the farm's private bucket
4. Back uppnpm story:push farm in the sibling ../uma-platform checkout, when it's there; skipped with a note when it isn't
5. SayThe release's number, its date and its note
  • The note is --note "…", else the first line of ship.md in the pack's folder, else none. It's one line of at most 160 characters, since the morning shows it; a longer one stops the ship before anything is uploaded.
  • On the phone: the phone picks the release up on its next open. The next morning on an existing farm opens New today with "New story arrived · your note", once a release, and Account shows Story: Release n, date. A new farm meets the story in its opening.
  • Nothing new, nothing published: a pack the bucket already plays keeps its number, and the drafts are still backed up. A ship that stopped partway (say, at the backup) is safe to run again.
  • --dry-run reads the bucket (the number, which files are there) and lists what it would upload, uploading nothing.
  • Rolling back: pnpm story:ship --release <n> publishes release n's index again, numbered anew, with the note "Rolled back to n". The bucket keeps every file and the last ten indexes, so any of those can come back.
  • The steps alone: pnpm story:release builds the release (any pack releases; it refuses only when there's no pack folder), and pnpm story:publish uploads it, unnumbered, so the morning doesn't announce it. The ship is the routine.

A complete Uma

The menu stays a menu: a partial Uma plays whole, and nothing waits on the story. But the story can also lay an Uma out whole, every piece the game reads for her, so it sees all of her at once, rewrites what it wants and ships her complete. pnpm story:scaffold writes the pieces she's missing from the default story, marked as the default's; pnpm story:status says, for each Uma, which pieces are hers, which are still the default and which are missing.

The checklist: 107 pieces, in the pack's own shapes (the rest of this page), and one more for each day world.json adds with a scene of its own; a festival's night counts too when she has one written. It's everything the game plays:

PartIts pieces
Her cast entry, cast.jsonher schedule (her home, job and routine together, one piece: what her profile and the calendar show, her life in town), chapters (her chapters)
Her bible, umas/<id>.jsonfavouriteLine, gifts, chats, townLines, mornings, meals, keepsake, petName, wedding, wishes, greetings, smallTalk, giftLines, and a beat for each of the 27 rungs in her route
Her route, routes/<id>.json65 events: her meeting (arrival), f1–f10, confession, r1–r10, proposal, wedding; invited-to-work, wedding-night, moving-in, s1–s5; each evening's scene (the six hangout-<kind>, the eleven date-<kind>, dinner-in-town, a-night-in); and every occasion a route can write (her occasions): new-year; the five festivals' days (flower-show, fishing-derby, cooking-contest, animal-show, harvest-cup); birthday; anniversary; and each of the story's days' scenes (the game's six, the story's days, and any world.json adds). Then each festival's night she has written, an optional piece

A festival's night is optional: written (<festival>-night, or derby-night and harvest-moon for the Fishing Derby's and the Cooking Contest's), it plays and counts as hers, once under whichever of its ids is written; not written, the festival has no night scene, and that's never missing. The story's standard writes the Fishing Derby's, the Cooking Contest's and the Harvest Cup's.

The marker, "draft": "default": a piece that still holds the default story's words.

  • Where it sits: on the piece itself when it's an object: an event, a beat, a chapter, her job, each routine stop, her chats, gifts, meals, keepsake and wedding. A piece that's a plain value or a list of lines (her home; her bible's favouriteLine, townLines, mornings, petName, wishes) can't hold a key, so its marker sits on its file's own object: "draft": { "mornings": "default" } on her bible, "draft": { "home": "default" } on her cast entry. Her routine and her chapters are the default while every stop or chapter is marked, and her schedule while each of her home, job and routine that's there is marked (any one unmarked makes it hers).
  • It changes nothing in play. The game reads a marked piece as written, and a marked pack plays exactly as the same pack unmarked; a release carries the marker as it is.
  • The check counts it: a marked piece is "default, not hers yet". Rewrite a piece, then remove its marker: a marked piece whose words aren't the default's is a note, "rewritten? remove the marker". A draft with any other value is a note too, and the piece counts as hers.
{ "id": "f3", "draft": "default", "steps": [{ "narrate": "You share a snack on the fence rail." }] }
{ "characterId": "9000001", "mornings": ["“I’ll put the kettle on. Don’t move.”"], "draft": { "mornings": "default" } }

What the scaffold writes, from the default story rendered for her:

  • Her words: her name from master data (a sister's from her cast entry), her favourite good and her line's work as the game fills them; {farmer} stays the placeholder. Her temperament (her talks' and her mornings' lines) is her bible's own temperament when it gives one, else her outfit's from master data, else steady, as the game plays.
  • Her chats: the default story's friend, sweetheart and married talks for her temperament, as her friend, partner and spouse sets. The sets are coarser than the default's six stages, so from Friendship 1 to 2, as a farmhand and while engaged she says her friend and partner lines, where the default had lines of their own.
  • Her schedule, when she has none: the game's own default schedule, the same function's output the game plays (scheduleOf, who's where), written as her routine, one stop marked default: her favourite spot (her spot; else the six in turn by her place in the cast) on every third day of each season, the first on day 1, 2 or 3 by her place in the cast. No home and no job: she's at home off the map her other days, so she plays exactly where she did. Give her a home, a job and days of her own as the story finds them, and remove the marker.
  • Everything else is the default as the game already plays it: her events and their pays, her chapters, her tastes, her mornings and meals, her town lines, her keepsake, her pet name (your name, {farmer}), her wedding's places, no wishes, and a beat with only its id for each rung; her occasions the default story has a scene for (New Year's, the five festivals' days, her birthday), and the one festival's night it has, the Fishing Derby's, as derby-night.
  • What the default story has no words for stays missing, hers to write: her greetings, smallTalk and giftLines; her anniversary; and the story's days' scenes. So a scaffolded Uma is "missing pieces" until the story writes them, and "complete" means everything the game plays is there. The other festivals' nights are optional, so the scaffold writes none.

The World Book, and world.json

The world building reaches the game in two ways:

  • What the player sees: world.json in the pack, every part optional: the town's, the farm's and the region's names, the farm's history, the festivals, the story's days, each place's name and line, a line for each season, and Town notes. The world has its shape, and pnpm story:ship carries it with the pack.
  • What shapes the game: the World Book, content/story/world-book.md, kept up to date and shipped with every pack (it travels with story:push farm). It holds:
    • the world bible and its history;
    • every character's background, the families and crews, the town and its places;
    • "For the game": what the story wants the game to support, one item each, with why.
  • Who reads it: the game's sessions read it before designing anything (pnpm story:pull farm), and the owner reads it on Uma Story's World page, https://uma-platform.web.app/story/farm/world (owner only), where "For the game" stands apart as "What the story asks of the game". story:push farm reads the drafts from ../uma-farm/content/story, or from STORY_FARM_DIR; story:ship sets it for you.
  • "For the game" is where the story asks. The game's side picks each item up into the roadmap or a packet, and once it's built, this menu says how to write for it. Group scenes, for the families and crews, are on the roadmap's long game (Phase 2).

What the game assumes

The owner's standing direction for anything that reaches the screen:

  • adults only, tender and never explicit;
  • the protagonist is a man (the owner, 2026-11-14): named by the player and never drawn, the player's eyes. Lines may say he, her husband, the groom;
  • a gift names a good the game has (goods/ids.ts); a new good is a request to the game (her bible's gifts.proposed), not a rule.

Privacy

  • The story names real franchise characters, so it never goes in git or in uma-art. content/story/ is ignored by git, and docs/story/ holds only original drafts, with no franchise names.
  • No character or outfit names in code, tests, docs, commit messages or pull request bodies. The private-content guard runs in CI.
  • Commit only tools or original drafts (docs/story/), never the private pack.
  • Backups, from uma-platform: pnpm story:push farm backs up the drafts (the ship runs it), and pnpm story:pull farm --drafts brings them to this Mac.

How changes reach the story

  • Each rule change updates this menu in the same commit as the code and the game mechanics, so this page is always what the game reads today.
  • The main session messages the story session when something it can use ships: a new field, a new scene to write, a new event id.

The words

Sisters

A sister is an outfit of an Uma, presented as a character of her own, with her own name and profile: the Uma Ranker's, named s<outfitId> (s and her outfit's id). The pack takes her id wherever it takes a character's: the cast, the retired welcome list, a tie's a or b, her bible umas/s<outfitId>.json, her route routes/s<outfitId>.json and a pair's file.

  • Her face, bust, talents and temperament are her outfit's, from master data: an outfitId on her entry is ignored (a note).
  • Her name is her entry's name, from the pack's private release at run time, never git. Without one, she shows as her elder's sister (a note).
  • She lives in town and is out there like everyone else, with her own home, job and routine if you give her them, and is met like anyone: by introducing yourself in town.

Her life in town

Every Uma is a retired racer with a life in town: a home, a part-time job, the places she likes. Three optional fields on her cast entry say where she is, when: her schedule. They are exactly what the player sees: her profile's "Where to find her" (today, and this season's days and places), the calendar's "In town" on every day, and the town map's who's at each place now (who's where). Nothing is placed at random. Until hers is written, the game's default schedule plays: her favourite spot (spot) on every third day of each season, at home off the map the rest. Days are turns and nothing reads a clock, so "when" is days of the season and seasons:

The festivals

Each festival is { "id", "name", "season", "day", "happens" }, its day a season and a day of it, 1–12.

  • One of the game's five, by its id (flower-show, fishing-derby, cooking-contest, animal-show, harvest-cup): renamed by its name, moved by its season and day (each defaulting to the game's), and happens says what happens. It keeps its game rule under the story's name, wherever it falls: its festival night, the morning's news, a festival trigger or gate naming its id, and the contest its rank challenge counts (the Flower Show's, the Fishing Derby's, the Cooking Contest's, the Animal Show's) when the festivals are built. A moved one plays all of it on its new day, every year, and none on its old one. The game's five are never removed.
  • Any other id is one of the story's own, added: it needs its name, season and day. It's a festival day: the calendar marks it, the morning says it, festival night opens, and a festival trigger or gate may name its id. It has no contest.
  • One festival a day. The game's five take their days first (where the story puts them); a move onto a day another holds is skipped, and that festival keeps the game's day. Then the story's own, in the file's order: one on a day already held is skipped.
  • The calendar lists each with its happens under it.

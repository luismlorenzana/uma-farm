The game's design

Second pass: the owner's rework (2026-11-14), with the owner's answers folded in. What the farm is to play, rebuilt around the owner's input: - a landscape game with a farm map and a navigable town; - a dialogue engine with busts, faces and outfits; - a day of limited actions, and a farming loop in the spirit of the classic farm-life games; - Umas who take the farm's work over and get better at it; - upkeep and loans; - a calendar you can see; - the Uma loop from meeting to married life; - a default Uma, so every character plays fully before her story and art land.

It builds on what exists wherever it can, and says plainly where something is replaced. It names no character and none of the story's places. How each built system works today is in the game mechanics. The page view is game-design.html.

The game in one line

You take over a farm gone to weed in a quiet valley. Day by day you work it with your own hands, meet the retired racers who've made the valley their home, bring them onto the farm, where they take the work over and grow better at it, and fall in love, until your days belong to the people more than the fields.

Pillars

PillarWhat it means in play
Your day is preciousA day holds a fixed number of actions. Every farm task and every outing costs one. What you spend them on is the game
From fields to peopleAt first you do the farm's work yourself. As Umas join the farm they take it over, and your actions turn to the valley: talks, gifts, hangouts, dates
Practice makes her betterEvery Uma can do every task, and the more she does one the better she gets at it: more yield, better quality, fewer actions
Her life is her ownEvery Uma is a retired racer with a life in town: a place, a part-time job, goals of her own (the story's). The farm is one part of her day
A farm that must pay its wayBuildings and residents cost upkeep. Loans let you build ahead, and the daily work pays them back
Always completeEvery Uma plays her whole story from day one, from a default the game provides, replaced piece by piece as her story and art arrive

The screens (landscape)

The whole game turns to landscape: the phone held sideways.

ScreenWhat it is
The farmThe farm as a map you look across: fields as tiles you till, plant, water and harvest; the coop and barn; the woods' edge and the pond; the mine's mouth; the buildings. Tap a tile or a place to act on it
The townA town map you move around: the plaza, the market, the café, the harbour, the library, the hilltop track, and the homes and spots the story adds. Each location shows who's there now; tap one to interact
A sceneThe dialogue engine: her bust in the outfit the moment calls for, her face changing line by line, choices at the bottom
The calendarThe season's days at a glance: festivals, birthdays, her events, with the faces of the Umas who have something that day
The farm's bookMoney, upkeep and loans; the work board; the people; the Diary

A thin bar is always visible: the day and season, the actions left, and the gold.

On the look: - the farm map starts as a clean, readable tile map, built to be fast and clear for acting on single tiles; - the HD-2D stage comes later, when uma-platform's new look path reaches the bar the owner wants; - the old portrait HD-2D preview, and the programmer art drawn for it, retire.

A day: limited actions

  • The protagonist (a man, the player's own) has 10 actions a day to start. More come from the farm's upgrades and the household.
  • Free: looking. The map, the calendar, the book and menus cost nothing.
  • The day ends when the actions run out or you end it. The night settles everything, as it does today.
  • Special days give extra event actions: New Year's, the festivals, her birthday. On those days you can play several Umas' events past the usual budget, so a holiday's scenes all fit.

The farm loop

In the spirit of the classic farm-life games, with the owner's additions.

AreaYour actionsWhat the day does
The fieldsTill a tile, plant seeds, water it (every day), harvest itWatered crops grow a stage each night; dry ones don't; a season's end withers what's left
Animals (the coop and the barn)Feed, brush or care, collect eggs or milk or woolCared-for animals grow fond and give better goods; animals left unfed grow unhappy
FishingCast once at the pond or the bayThe season and the spot decide the catch
ForagingSearch the woods or the hills onceThe season's finds
MiningWork a floor's face once; the stairs go deeperOre, stone and gems, rarer with depth
StationsProcess a load: grain to flour, logs to planks, fish to fillets, ore to ingotsReady the next morning
ShippingPut goods in the binPaid that night

What's built and kept: - the crops and their seasons, the prices and stars; - the forest areas and fishing spots and their finds; - the stations' recipes, the bin and the night's settling; - building places by Farm Rank.

What changes: - the fields become tiles acted on one at a time, with daily watering, instead of blocks an Uma tends for days; - gathering becomes one action for one haul, instead of jobs that last days; - the ranch and the mine, never built, are built this way.

Umas at work

  • Inviting her: once you're friends, you can invite an Uma to work on the farm. She's then a farmhand.
  • Her day:
    • she keeps her own life: her part-time job in town (the story says what it is) comes first;
    • then she gives the farm a few actions a day, 3 to start, more as she settles in and once you're married;
    • the actions go to the tasks on the work board.
  • The work board: you set each farmhand's tasks: water these tiles, care for the coop, fish, mine. Every Uma can do every task.
  • Aptitude: each task has a level for each Uma that grows with practice. A higher level means more yield, better stars, a task done in fewer actions, and new tricks (watering a row at once).
    • What's kept: today's talents and rank points become the start: her stats set where each aptitude begins, and work raises it.
  • You're freed: as farmhands take the farm's tasks, the board fills, your own actions turn to the valley, and the game becomes about the people.

What's kept: - the whole romance: her ladders, points, events and plays, perks, evenings, the ring, the wedding, houses, the household, bonds between Umas; - her chapters, framing her story along these stages.

What changes: - meeting becomes introducing yourself, an action in town, with every Uma there from day one. There's no welcome week (the owner: "I will go to the Umas I want to meet"): nobody comes to the Gate on her own; - farmhand is a new stage between friend and sweetheart; - the wedding night and moving in become stages of their own.

The town

  • A town map, navigable in landscape: walk between locations by tapping them.
  • Who's where: each Uma has her own place and routine from the story: where she works, where she likes to be, when. Each location lists who's there now. With no routine written for her, the game places her by today's draw.
  • Tap an Uma for her menu. Each item costs an action:
    • introduce yourself;
    • talk;
    • give a gift;
    • invite to a hangout or a date;
    • invite to work on the farm;
    • her event, when it's ready.
  • The town's people, the shopkeepers and the mayor, are at their posts, with their own menus: buy seeds, sell, build, borrow.

The dialogue engine

  • Every scene (meetings, talks, events, hangouts, dates, festivals, the wedding) plays through one engine:
    • her bust in the right outfit;
    • her face changing with each line's emote;
    • the speaker's name;
    • choices;
    • for some scenes, a backdrop of the place.
  • The art:
    • the pilot is complete: 12 outfit portraits and 15 faces each, for the two Umas with full art;
    • each face sits over each outfit's portrait;
    • neutral is the portrait's own face;
    • a face the art hasn't drawn yet shows its nearest one.
  • Outfits by context: the scene's own outfit when the story names one, else the moment's:
    • work clothes at work;
    • the season's outfit on an ordinary day;
    • the garden party dress at the spring festival, a picnic or a tea;
    • the kimono on festival nights and New Year's;
    • the dinner dress at dinners;
    • the wedding dress, then the wedding night outfit;
    • pajamas at night;
    • the swimsuit at the summer shore.
  • Special events can set any outfit.
  • Her big moments, full screen: meeting her, a day at the beach, the confession, the wedding, the wedding night, a quiet evening in and the morning after open onto her illustration, the text box over its lower edge. Each one seen is kept in a gallery on her profile.

The economy

  • Income: the bin's sales each night, and the stations' goods sold.
  • Upkeep: each building, each Dorm room and each resident costs a little gold each day. The book shows the daily total against an average day's income, so the farm has to grow its work to grow its people.
    • Short of upkeep: a warning first. Then the farm runs at half pace (fewer actions for farmhands) until it's paid. No one leaves, and nothing is lost.
  • Loans: borrow from the town (the mayor's office) to build a place or a house ahead of the gold. Repaid a little each day from the night's income. One loan at a time, then two as the Farm Rank grows; a small fee.
  • Goals: each loan is a goal: the projects list shows what's owed and when it's clear.

The calendar

  • Always in reach: a calendar screen, and the bar's date opens it.
  • Every day shows:
    • its festival;
    • birthdays;
    • the occasion scenes the story wrote for it (New Year's, the festival nights, the flower show);
    • the faces of the Umas who have an event that day.
  • More events:
    • the story's holidays and customs (the year's first bell, market days, the valley's minor days) from the world file;
    • weather days later.
  • Special days lift the action budget for events (above).

The default Uma

Story packs and art will take a long time to land, so every Uma plays fully before hers does:

  • A blank bust and profile:
    • the bust: a soft silhouette of an Uma (ears, hair, shoulders) in each outfit's colours;
    • the face: a simple face for each of the 22 expressions;
    • the profile: a plain profile card;
    • all drawn by Claude in code, and replaced the moment her art is published.
  • A default story, complete:
    • talks for every stage, by her temperament;
    • reactions to gifts;
    • hangouts and dates;
    • an event for every step from meeting to married life, the proposal and the wedding night included;
    • festival and birthday scenes;
    • married life's events.

These are written once for the game, warm and short, with her name, her favourite things and her temperament filled in. The protagonist is a man. They're replaced piece by piece as her story pack lands. - This builds on what's there: today's placeholders become this default, much richer.

Settled with the owner (2026-11-14)

  1. The protagonist is a man. The story's old rule (the farmer never gendered, every line "you") is lifted: lines may say he, her husband, the groom. He's still never drawn: the player's eyes.
  2. No welcome week. You go to the Umas you want to meet; nobody comes to the Gate on her own. The opening ends with the farm, not a visitor.
  3. Everything that takes an action is in the table above, and married life adds cooking meals and the night's companion, who greets you in the morning.
  4. The numbers: 10 actions a day for the protagonist, 3 for a farmhand, more once married, and a little daily upkeep per building and resident, all tuned by playing.
  5. The farm map's look: a clean tile map now, the HD-2D look when uma-platform's look path is ready.

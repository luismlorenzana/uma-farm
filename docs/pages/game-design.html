The game's design

A first pass, for the owner to rework (2026-11-14). What the farm is to play: the fantasy, what you do in a sitting, a day, a season and a year, how the farm and the Umas feed each other, and what the first real season holds. It draws on the story's World Book, which stays private: this page names no character and none of the story's places. How each system works in detail is in the game mechanics; what's next to build is in the roadmap. The page view is game-design.html.

The game in one line

You take over a farm gone to weed in a quiet valley, and the Umas who come there to start over help you bring it back, while you fall in love with one of them, then build a home with her, and with the others who stay.

The fantasy

  • A second life, for everyone. Racing is years behind the Umas. Each comes to the valley for a reason of her own: rest, a trade to learn, a person, the quiet. You're a newcomer too, which is why they open up to you.
  • A place that remembers. The farm took in racing stables every summer, and the valley still runs its own race on the old hilltop track at the year's end. Bringing the farm back brings the valley's old life back with it.
  • Tender, never explicit. Grown women, warm stories, fades to morning. The farmer is "you": never drawn, never gendered.

Pillars

PillarWhat it means in play
Her story firstEvery Uma has her own chapters, from meeting to married life. The farm's goals are her story's next steps ("Opens when the Quarry is built")
A farm that grows with youNo global plot. Income and goods raise the Farm Rank; the rank offers places; you build them. Each new place is new work, new goods and a door in someone's story
A valley of everyoneEvery character lives in town from day 1. You choose whom to talk to; her story begins when you've met her her way
Short sittings, a long lifeA day is a turn you end when you like: a few minutes on a phone. Seasons, festivals and years give it a rhythm
Always playableThe story arrives over time; anything unwritten still plays and pays. Nothing waits for a finished book

A sitting: about five minutes on the phone

 the morning ──► the work board ──► the valley ──► the evening ──► End the day
 what happened    who works where    talk, gift,     one hangout     the night settles
 overnight,       today; collect     meet, visit     or date, if     work, growth, the
 who's new,       what's ready;      a spot; her     someone's free  bin's gold, the
 a new chapter    build if you can   event if Ready                  rank, and who's met
  1. The morning tells you what the night did: the bin's gold, the harvest, a place built, a Farm Rank reached, who met you, a new chapter, a new story arrived.
  2. The work board: each Uma on the farm takes a job (the Fields, the woods, the pond, a station) or tends a block of the Fields. Jobs take days; Ready work is collected with a tap. A building you can afford is one tap away.
  3. The valley: the Gate's visitor in the first week; the town's spots, where a few of everyone are out each day; a talk or a gift; her heart event when it's Ready (one a day for each Uma).
  4. The evening: one each day, with someone free: a hangout from early friendship, a date once you're together.
  5. End the day: the night settles everything. Nothing happens while the app is closed.

The rhythm

ScaleWhat it holds
A dayOne turn: the morning, the board, the valley, the evening, the night
A week (the first)The welcome week: one Uma at the Gate each morning, each with her first chapter on the farm as it starts
A season (12 days)Its crops and catches, its festival on the 10th, its own line in the morning; a season's last day withers what isn't harvested
A year (48 days)Four festivals and the valley's race on the year's last day; birthdays, anniversaries, New Year's from the second year
The long gameEvery place built, every house, the household's years, and as many of the valley's stories as you choose to follow

  • The farm pays for the romance: a place built is often the gate to her next chapter, and a ring needs the farm's ingots and gems.
  • The romance pays the farm: the Umas are the farm's hands; each level of hers brings a perk at her line's work; a spouse's breakfast feeds the farm; each wedding opens a house, and the houses open the homestead's rows.
  • The town ties them: you meet the valley in town, and the town's own people mark each new place: a surveyor walks the new mine, the smith lights the forge, the cook is the restaurant's first guest.

The first real season: what "playable" means

The target for the game's first good version. Every piece below has to be real: your cast and story, real busts, a farm screen that looks right.

DaysWhat happens
1Your name; the opening's four lines (the farm's last keeper hands you the key); the first Uma waiting at the Gate. She's the farm's first helper. Plant the carrots
2–5One welcomer each morning, each a scene at the Gate that nods at the mornings before it. The work board fills; the woods and the pond open up
3–9The town: talks at its six spots; the first Umas met by their own triggers; the first heart events; the first evenings
≈ 6–9The first build: the Mill. The Diary's projects show what's next
10The spring festival: a garden party, a judged entry, the first outings of the year
11–12The season's end: what's not harvested withers; summer's line in the morning; Farm Rank 2–3

At the end of the first season you should have: - the welcome five on the farm, and a few more met; - one or two Umas in their second chapter; - the Mill and maybe the Sawmill built; - a clear next goal the story points at.

Questions to settle in the rework

These shape everything below them, so they come first:

  1. Who are you to them? Today you can marry more than one Uma: each wedding opens a house. Is that the game: one true love, or a household of several?
  2. What's the pressure? There's no energy, no deadline and no failure: a day ends when you say. Is a gentle limit wanted (hours in a day, a season's goals), or is the calm the point?
  3. How much farming? The farm can be a light frame for the stories or a deep game of its own (the workshops' minigames, expeditions). Which matters more in a sitting?
  4. How many stories at once? A hundred characters in town, five welcomers fully written. Is the game about a few deep routes or many light ones?
  5. What does a year end with? The valley's race on the year's last day could be the yearly finale. Is it a competition, a celebration or a story beat?
  6. What's the look? The 2D map, the HD-2D stage, or something else: which matters most for the game to feel finished?

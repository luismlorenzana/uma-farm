Her look: outfits and expressions

A plan, for the owner's go (2026-11-13). Each Uma gets a wardrobe (her own outfit, four seasonal outfits, a kimono, a dinner dress, a wedding dress and pajamas) and busts that change expression in dialogue. The bust is composed in the game from layers: the outfit's body, her head, and an expression's face. The page view is her-look.html.

Busts that change expression

The layers

A bust is six layers, back to front. Every layer is a full canvas, already in place, so the game only stacks them.

#LayerOne perHolds
1back (optional)OutfitWhat sits behind her: a veil's back, a hood, hair ornaments behind the head
2bodyOutfitShoulders, chest, arms and neck, in that outfit
3headUma, or an outfit that restyles her hairHer face's skin and nose, the back of her hair, and her ears: no eyes, brows or mouth
4faceUma × expressionEyes, brows, mouth, and the expression's marks: blush, tears, sweat
5hairUma, or an outfit that restyles her hairHer bangs and the locks that fall over her face, so they sit over the eyes and brows
6front (optional)OutfitWhat sits over her hair: a hat, a kanzashi, a veil's front, a nightcap
  • One head pose per Uma: the head never moves between outfits or expressions, so every face fits every body. Only the body below the neck changes with the outfit.
  • An outfit may restyle her hair (an updo for the kimono, loose hair for pajamas) with its own head and hair, drawn in the same pose, so the faces still fit.
  • The game composes them: it stacks the layers, caches the result by its parts' hashes, and swaps only the face when the expression changes. Any missing layer falls back: no face for that expression means her nearest one (the table below), and no layers at all means her single master-data bust.

The expressions

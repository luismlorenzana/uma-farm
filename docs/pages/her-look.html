Her look: outfits, expressions and illustrations

Each Uma has a standard wardrobe of 12 outfits and a dialogue menu of 22 expressions. The engine shows her portrait in the outfit the moment calls for, with her face changing line by line. Her big moments open onto full-screen illustrations. ChatGPT draws them (character art), and the game composes them. How the game plays them is the dialogue engine; what's drawn next is the art brief, below. The page view is her-look.html.

From the character art

uma-platform's Uma Character Art (https://uma-platform.web.app/art) holds, for each Uma:

  • master data's face, bust and chibi sprite set;
  • the full-body 3D render (outfit-renders, 768×1024);
  • the universe looks: ChatGPT's owner-approved pictures of an Uma in another world, Farm Life among them.

Farm Life is where the farm's art comes from: twelve Umas have their dialogue portraits (12 outfits and 15 faces each) and all nine of their illustrations. Master data carries them to the game, with no farm release.

Busts that change expression

A portrait per outfit, a face per expression

  • The portrait: her head and shoulders in one outfit, with her calm neutral face. One for each outfit.
  • The face: just her face in one expression, drawn to sit exactly over the face of any of her portraits. One for each expression.
  • Why one set of faces fits every outfit: her head is the same in every portrait (same size, spot, angle and bangs). Only what's below the neck, and what she wears on her head, changes with the outfit.
  • The game composes them: the portrait, with the face over it, swapped when the expression changes. Both are cached on the device (her portraits).
  • Fallbacks:
    • a face not drawn yet shows its fallback (the table below), ending at the portrait's own neutral face;
    • an outfit not drawn yet shows her spring portrait;
    • an Uma with none shows the blank bust in the outfit's colours, with its own 22 faces; her master-data bust stays on cards and lists.

The matrix uses 50 local authored Farm routes and 76 pair files, checked against the default scenes and scene players. The wedding-night routes call for loving or flustered on 153 of 171 spoken steps. The a-night-in scenes call for tender, laughing or sleepy on 126 of 164 spoken steps. The baseline pajamas also appear in scenes with more emotional range, including nighttime heart events. A named calendar anniversary scene normally wears dinner. The five numbered anniversary-night looks belong to the separate married-life player.

When a scene calls for more. Start with the actual fallback. dreamy and sleepy can use tender, teasing can use happy, and worried can use sad. Approve the exact composition when it supplies the punchline or changes the mood. Add sleepy to a numbered look when a future wake-up scene explicitly asks for it. A splash illustration carries its own pose and expression.

How to apply it in Facial Expressions. Use the row as the per-base outputs approval checklist. Keep at least one approved non-neutral composition per new base so the portrait packer has an explicit face position. Carry forward existing approvals. The current release packs one shared layer per expression for the character, so other shared expressions remain available in the game when a scene requests them. The matrix sets the preview and approval workload. Numbered wardrobe art also needs a slot-to-portrait mapping before it appears in the game. The married-life story contract lists the current story keys.

The nine additional pajama looks and five anniversary looks call for 28 previews per Uma. At the standard 16 states per outfit, those same 14 looks would call for 224 previews.

The art brief

For the character-art session (ChatGPT), standing guidance for each Uma's dialogue and big moments.

Where it stands: twelve Umas have their portraits (12 outfits and 15 faces each) and all nine of their illustrations. Until an Uma has hers, the game plays her on the blank bust, so nothing waits on the art.

What to draw

For each Uma:

  • a bust in each outfit: head and shoulders, in the style of her full character art, with a calm face;
  • shared facial expressions for the outfits and scenes in the approval matrix;
  • her illustrations, one for each big moment (below).

Draw her the same way in every outfit, with the same pose and angle, so her faces can be reused across outfits. Every Uma is drawn as an adult, and every picture is tasteful: tender, never explicit.

The expressions to draw

Each one is a feeling the story will ask for, so each should read at a glance, small on a phone, and stay in her character: a reserved Uma's joy is quieter than a bright one's.

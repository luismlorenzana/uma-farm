The road to a full game

Start here. This page says what the farm is, where it stands, what "a full game" means for it, and the phases that get it there. How the game works today is in game-mechanics.md, the farm's progression in the farm's progress. How it's built is in technical/architecture.md, and the cloud in technical/infrastructure.md. The page view is pages/roadmap.html.

What this game is

  • Personal, never public. It's the owner's own game, on the owner's iPhone, reachable only with the owner's Google account. Nothing here plans for other players, stores or distribution.
  • The owner's real game, built up in place. The deployed site is the game the owner plays, never a sandbox: no testing tools, cheats, jump-ahead shortcuts or debug panels. Each new piece lands in the game itself, long paths are checked by the domain's simulations and local runs, and the owner only plays.
  • The farm is kept. Save v5 is the farm the owner plays and keeps. A later change grows v5 in place, or migrates it: no more start-overs. The server keeps older saves in saveBackups.
  • Built whole, played live. Every system plays from day one. Where the story hasn't reached yet, the default story plays, and where the art hasn't, the blank bust. The story arrives over time in packs, and each pack replaces the default piece by piece. No pack has to be complete.
  • The story is free. The story session builds the world, the backgrounds and the characters however it wants. Much of it may never appear in the game; it informs the dialogue that does. The game asks the story for nothing it can't play without (how the story reaches the game).
  • No global chapters, no farm-wide story. Each Uma's story is hers, in her own chapters, from the day you meet her to married life.
  • Your day is precious. A day is ten actions, and the farm is worked by hand at first. As the Umas you invite to work take the farm's tasks over, your own day turns to the people.
  • The farm grows by what you earn and grow. The Farm Rank, from income and farmed goods, says what you may build, and you build it with gold and goods, or on a loan from the mayor's office. What you build costs upkeep each night, so the farm has to pay its way. The homestead grows with your life with the Umas: the n-th wedding opens house n.
  • A lean start, then the town. You name your farmer, read a few lines, and you're on the farm. Every Uma lives in town from day one, and you meet whoever you choose by introducing yourself there. Nobody comes to the Gate on her own.

The finish line

The farm is a full game when all eight of these hold:

  1. One build, one look, no testing tools. No build flags, no preview switch, no cheats or debug panels. The tile farm is Home, and the HD-2D farm is retired. This holds today.
  2. Everything live, filled by the story over time. Every system plays with the default story and the blank bust, and each story pack and each Uma's art replace what they bring. This holds today.
  3. The town of everyone. Every Uma lives in town, and you meet whoever you choose by introducing yourself. This holds today.
  4. Each Uma's story to its end, and the farm built out. Her chapters play from meeting her to married life, its meals and the night's companion. Every place can be built, and the homestead fills with houses. The game keeps going: years, festivals, the household, records.
  5. Every active place works. The ranch, the Mine and Quarry, the Kitchen, the sea, expeditions and the Restaurant are in. The four Festival ground contests are complete.
  6. Real art everywhere. Every Uma's portraits and illustrations, finished frames, and farm-work animations.
  7. It feels finished. A quick start, places that explain themselves the first time, and pacing proven by simulation and by your play.
  8. It's safe and steady. The farm's save grows in place, with a tested restore and error reports that reach us.

Phase 1: One game, built around the Umas (done)

Goal, met: the deployed game is one game: built whole, played live, its farm growing by building, its people met in town, each with her own story.

  • The game's frame: one build, the default story and the blank bust wherever the story and the art haven't reached, the farm growing by building, the lean start, the town, meeting her, and her chapters.
  • The story's way in: pnpm story:ship checks, releases, publishes and backs up in one command, and the morning says "New story arrived". world.json's names, history, festivals, places, seasons and Town notes reach the screen (the world in the pack). The World Book, with its "For the game", is read on Uma Story's World page. How it all works is the story pack's first part.
  • The owner's rework (2026-11-14): a landscape game with a day of actions, a classic farm-life loop on a tile farm, Umas who take the farm over and get better at it, a navigable town, a dialogue engine, upkeep and loans, a visible calendar, and a default Uma so every character plays fully. The design is design/game-design.md (page), and all ten of its steps are built (F74–F85), with her illustrations and the loose ends. Where we are says what each one is.
  • Her look folded into the dialogue engine, and the HD-2D farm retired with landscape: the tile farm is Home.

Phase 2: The whole farm

Goal: every place you can build has its full game.

2.1, the ranch, and 2.2, the Mine and the Quarry, came with the rework. The farm at scale is in (the owner, 2026-11-15; the analysis): the field's own screen in plots (F89), food from day one with cooking in batches (F90), and work at scale (F91): up to eight plots, the stations' levels, thin hauls past four a place a day, and a market that feels a flood. A simulation at 10, 20 and 50 Umas, each on her farm days, shows two thirds of them on the farm a day, all in real jobs with real work still waiting (the farm at scale). The workshops came next, the Kitchen first (the owner's go, 2026-11-15): 2.3 is done: its stove, recipes, dishes and staff meal (F92), and the Cooking Contest on Autumn 10, the first festival you play (F93) (the Kitchen, the Cooking Contest). Before the sea (the owner, 2026-11-17): the launch cast is in (F96: the Ranker's Tier 1–5, 50 Umas, the launch cast), and so are farm days and home after the wedding (F95: a farmhand works the farm on the days her town life leaves free and eats her meals there; only a spouse moves in, into a room; the Dorm is retired: farmhands, the retired Dorm). The Dock and Boat open the sea, and the Expedition post sends farmhands out from Farm Rank 7 (expeditions).

Deferred

These items are outside the active roadmap and the finish criteria.

  • The Forge: Workshop gameplay and tool upgrades are deferred pending a redesign.
  • Sound: Music, sound effects, mute and volume.
  • Accessibility: The planned VoiceOver, motion, and text size pass.
  • Browser playthrough in CI: A headless browser playing the first days on every push.

Later: release packs

The game opens with the launch cast, the Uma Ranker's Tier 1–5: 50 Umas (F96, the owner, 2026-11-17). Later releases add sets of Umas as release packs. How a pack is defined (by the Ranker's tiers, or by hand), published (with master data or the story) and opened on a farm already playing is worked out when it starts.

Later: parcels to the other games

The farm sends items to uma-quest, uma-rpg and uma-racer, one way. It's parked until the farm's own game is done.

How we work from here

  • game-mechanics.md is what the game is. Every rule change updates it in the same commit, and its tables regenerate from the code (pnpm docs:update).
  • This roadmap is where the game is going. A finished step leaves it, and git keeps the history.
  • The real game, never a sandbox. No testing tools, cheats, jump-ahead shortcuts or debug panels. Long paths are checked by the domain's simulations and local runs; you only play.
  • Packets are for the game's work only. A step gets a packet in handoff/tasks/ when it's next, and the packet is deleted when it ships. handoff/README.md lists what's open now.
  • The story and the art have standing guides, not packets: how the story reaches the game, and the art brief for the next Umas.
  • There's no decision log. A commit says what changed and why. Your calls go into the roadmap or the mechanics document they change.

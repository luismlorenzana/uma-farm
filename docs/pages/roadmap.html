The road to a full game

Start here. This page says where the farm stands, what "a full game" means for it, and the phases that get it there. How the game works today is in game-mechanics.md. How it's built is in technical/architecture.md, and the cloud in technical/infrastructure.md. The page view is pages/roadmap.html.

Where we are: a sandbox

The farm runs on your phone every day, but it's still a sandbox: several tracks were started side by side, and none is finished.

What makes it a sandbox: - three ways to run it: the deployed build, the romance build, and the HD-2D switch; - stand-ins wherever the art or the story isn't ready; - a story written in a separate tool; - one chapter of content.

The finish line

The farm is a full game when all seven of these hold:

  1. One build, one look. No build flags, no preview switch. The HD-2D farm is Home, and the 2D Hub is retired.
  2. The story is in. All 20 Umas arrive, with their routes, heart events, confessions, weddings and married life, from your story. No stand-in pack outside the tests.
  3. A whole arc. Chapters 1–5 play start to finish, there's an ending, and the game keeps going after it: seasons and years, festivals, the household, records.
  4. Every place works. The ranch, the Mine and the Quarry, the Kitchen with its cooking game, the Cooking Contest, expeditions, and the Restaurant's and the Forge's games.
  5. Real art and sound everywhere. No cast standees, no stand-in frames, farm-work animations, music and sound effects.
  6. It feels finished. A first run that teaches by playing, settings (sound, motion, text size), every control labelled and easy to tap, and pacing proven by simulation and by your play.
  7. It's safe and steady. A save that never loses progress, error reports that reach us, a full playthrough tested on every push, and a release routine with a check on your phone before production.

The audience gate

Who is the full game for? Decide this before Phase 5. Phases 1–4 are the same either way.

  • Recommended: a full game for you, private. It stays what it is today: your farm, on your phone, behind your sign-in.
  • Public, later, only by choice. The game is built on Umamusume: Pretty Derby. Its Umas, outfits, names and the art drawn from them belong to Cygames, so a public game needs an original cast and art in their place. It also needs accounts for many players, a privacy policy and terms, costs that grow with players, and support. Phase 5 lists that work.

The phases

Phase 0  Clean slate            ── done with this change
Phase 1  One game, not three    ── the story live, the HD-2D farm as Home, no switches
Phase 2  The whole farm         ── the workshops, chapters 2–5, the ending and the long game
Phase 3  Feel and polish        ── sound, animation, first run, settings, balance, speed
Phase 4  Production-ready       ── error reports, save safety, a tested playthrough, releases
Phase 5  Public (only by choice) ── an original cast, many players, legal, costs, distribution

Phases 1 and 2 can run as two tracks at once, one piece at a time each, as the romance and the HD-2D farm do now. Phase 3 starts once a workshop stands on the stage. Phase 4's error reports and save safety can come any time; they're small.

StepWhat

Later: parcels to the other games

The farm sends items to uma-quest, uma-rpg and uma-racer, one way. It's parked until the farm's own game is done.

How we work from here

  • game-mechanics.md is what the game is. Every rule change updates it in the same commit, and its tables regenerate from the code (pnpm docs:update).
  • This roadmap is where the game is going. A finished step leaves it, and git keeps the history.
  • Packets are for work in progress only. A step gets a packet in handoff/tasks/ when it starts, and the packet is deleted when it ships. handoff/README.md lists what's open now.
  • There's no decision log. A commit says what changed and why. Your calls go into the roadmap or the mechanics document they change.

The road to a full game

Start here. This page says where the farm stands, what "a full game" means for it, and the phases that get it there. How the game works today is in game-mechanics.md. How it's built is in technical/architecture.md, and the cloud in technical/infrastructure.md. The page view is pages/roadmap.html.

What this game is

  • Personal, never public. It's the owner's own game, on the owner's iPhone, reachable only with the owner's Google account. Nothing here plans for other players, stores or distribution.
  • Built whole, played live. Every system ships live and plays from day one, with plain placeholders wherever the story hasn't reached yet. The story arrives over time in packs, and each pack unlocks more of what the game already has: characters' lines, scenes, events and routes. No pack has to be complete, and nothing waits for "the story to be done".
  • The story is free. The story session builds the world, the backgrounds and the characters however it wants. Much of it may never appear in the game; it informs the dialogue that does. The game asks the story for nothing it can't play without: everything a pack holds is optional, and anything missing falls back to a placeholder.
  • A lean start, then the town. You name your farmer, read a few lines, and you're on the farm. Every character lives in town from the start. You choose who to talk to, and a character's story begins when you meet her trigger. The story's welcome list (five by default) comes to you early, so the first friends and helpers arrive in the first days.

The finish line

The farm is a full game when all eight of these hold:

  1. One build, one look. No build flags, no preview switch. The HD-2D farm is Home, and the 2D Hub is retired.
  2. Everything live, filled by the story over time. Every system plays with placeholders, and each story pack adds what it has. There's no "story complete" gate.
  3. The town of everyone. Every character lives in town. You choose who to talk to, and her story begins when her trigger is met.
  4. A whole arc. Chapters 1–5 play start to finish, there's an ending, and the game keeps going after it: seasons and years, festivals, the household, records.
  5. Every place works. The ranch, the Mine and the Quarry, the Kitchen with its cooking game, the Cooking Contest, expeditions, and the Restaurant's and the Forge's games.
  6. Real art and sound everywhere. No cast standees, no stand-in frames, farm-work animations, music and sound effects.
  7. It feels finished. A start that's quick, places that explain themselves the first time, settings (sound, motion, text size), every control labelled and easy to tap, and pacing proven by simulation and by your play.
  8. It's safe and steady. A save that never loses progress, error reports that reach us, a full playthrough tested on every push, and a check on your phone before each release.

The phases

Phase 0  Clean slate                  ── done
Phase 1  Live, with the story's frame ── an open story pack, placeholders, the lean start, the town, triggers, go live
Phase 2  The whole farm               ── the workshops, chapters 2–5, the ending and the long game
Phase 3  Feel and polish              ── sound, animation, settings, balance, speed
Phase 4  Steady                       ── error reports, save safety, a tested playthrough, releases

Phase 1 runs as two tracks, one piece at a time each: the story's frame, and the HD-2D farm. Phase 3 starts once a workshop stands on the stage. Phase 4's error reports and save safety are small and can come any time.

Later: parcels to the other games

The farm sends items to uma-quest, uma-rpg and uma-racer, one way. It's parked until the farm's own game is done.

How we work from here

  • game-mechanics.md is what the game is. Every rule change updates it in the same commit, and its tables regenerate from the code (pnpm docs:update).
  • This roadmap is where the game is going. A finished step leaves it, and git keeps the history.
  • Packets are for the game's work only. A step gets a packet in handoff/tasks/ when it's next, and the packet is deleted when it ships. handoff/README.md lists what's open now. The story has no packet of requirements: F40 only says how its work reaches the game.
  • There's no decision log. A commit says what changed and why. Your calls go into the roadmap or the mechanics document they change.

Your steps now

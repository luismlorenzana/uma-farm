kit-farm: the farm kit's contract

The contract kit-farm is drawn to: the full-colour 2D art the HD-2D stage builds the farm from, in uma-quest's kit format. Claude draws it in code from this doc, as a uma-art family, in two waves (F9). Wave 1 has shipped (uma-art 2.27.0) and the farm draws it; wave 2 is open. Until a frame ships, the stage's stand-in kit draws it in flat colours under the same name. Visual page: kit-farm.html.

It rests on: - uma-quest's kit spec, kits.md (the format: atlas, roles, sizes, style, delivery). The link is its last version: uma-quest has since retired the doc, and the code, uma-stage's, is the final word; - the HD-2D farm as it plays (game mechanics); - the farm as built: farm/places.ts's 23 places and romance/homestead.ts's homestead; - the openings uma-stage 0.2.1 made for a farm (its README).

The short answer

  • One kit, kit-farm, of a new kind farm, in uma-quest's format:
    • 128-texel tiles;
    • a camera at 35° from the south that never turns, so only tops and south faces are drawn;
    • props as upright cards;
    • full colour, lit from the top front-left.
  • It's drawn for the farm as it plays today: 23 places on 8 rows, plus the homestead's up to 3 rows, laid out portrait, 9 tiles across: three plots of 3 × 3 tiles a row.
  • Buildings are built, not painted whole: a building's front is put together from modular wall, window, door and roof pieces. There are three wall materials (plank, plaster, stone) and four roof colours (red, thatch, shingle, slate). One or two landmark props tell each place apart: the Mill's sails, the Forge's anvil.
  • Places don't change with work: the Umas at their slots and the kit's Ready bubbles show what's happening. Only crops grow, and they're drawn by family (12 ripe shapes for 27 crops), not one by one.
  • A place that isn't open yet is an overgrown lot: weeds, brambles and a broken fence, cleared when it opens. That's the farm's own opening, "The Overgrown Farm", and it lets wave 1 ship without the later places' pieces.
  • Two waves:
    • wave 1 (87 frames, shipped) is everything chapter 1 opens, and the romance's homestead: the Fields, the forest, the Fishery, the three stations, the yard, the Shop and the homestead;
    • wave 2 (30 frames, open) is the places the later chapters open: the ranch, the Mine and the Quarry, the Smelter and the Cutter, the Kitchen, the Restaurant, the Forge, the board, the festival ground and the expedition post.

1. The farm it's drawn for (KF1, KF2)

KF1: one kit, kind farm. kit-farm ships as a uma-art family in uma-quest's format (kits.md §2–§5), with four openings the farm needed of the shared stage, shipped in uma-stage 0.2.1 (its README): 1. A third kind, farm, with its own required list (§6). 2. A set on building pieces, so a design can pick a building's material. 3. Doors two columns wide (256 × 256). 4. Mounted pieces placed by the design at a level and column on a front, beyond the town format's sign beside the door.

Everything else is kits.md as written: - tile 128; - one level = one tile high, a wall = two levels; - variants picked by a position hash; - 2 px between frames; - foot, blocks and light on props.

KF2: the layout, portrait, 9 tiles across:

   ▒ = not open yet (a wave-2 place): drawn as an overgrown lot until it opens (KF7)
  • Rows are today's map rows (FARM_MAP_ROWS; game mechanics), gather → process → make from left to right. The homestead's rows sit between the yard and the town.
  • A band is 4 tiles deep: a 3 × 3 plot for each place, then a path row. The farm is 32 to 44 tiles tall.
  • In each plot, a building stands on the back two tiles, facing south onto the apron, the front row of tiles where its slots are and the Umas stand.
  • Levels: the farm's ground is level 1. The pond and the quarry pit are level 0, so their banks are risers from the geometry with no edge art (kits.md: edges and corners come from the geometry, not autotile masks). The Mine's cliff is a wall along its plot's back.
  • The farm's layout code sets the exact tiles: the lanes between columns, the Gate's opening and the path network (game mechanics).
  • Two things the stage handles, not the kit:
    • at 35° a one-storey roof hides about 4 tiles behind it (a two-storey about 7, the Gate's arch about 5), so 4-tile bands hide the band above's apron and path; the stage fades what stands in front (game mechanics);
    • 9 tiles (1,152 px at one texel a pixel) overflow a 2× phone (750 to 828 px); the stage fits the farm to the phone's width.

Neither changes a frame in this contract.

2. Buildings (KF3, KF4)

KF3: fronts from modular sets. A building is a box the builder makes: - its south face, from building-wall, window and door pieces, one level a module; - a roof slope, from roof pieces.

3. Work, crops and locked places (KF5–KF7)

KF5: places keep one look; the Umas show the work. The Umas make the farm move (game mechanics): an Uma walks in, stands at her slot with an emote, waves when she's done, and a Ready bubble rises over her. A place therefore needs no empty, working and ready art of its own. The kit draws each place once, and adds: - crates, barrel and sack as the yard's stock; - live lights (the furnace, the forge's chimney, the lanterns), which glow at night (the evenings); - crops, the one thing that grows on the map (KF6).

KF6: crops by family. The Fields' plots each hold a crop at a stage. The kit draws: - three shared stages: crop-sprout, crop-growing, and crop-withered (a crop left at a season's end, game mechanics); - twelve ripe families, each a 128-wide card standing on its plot's tile.

The crop's own icon (uma-art's items) shows in the Ready bubble and the Fields' sheet, so a family never hides which crop it is.

KF7: a place that isn't open is an overgrown lot. Its plot is drawn as grass with weeds-1, weeds-2, brambles and a fence-broken, placed by the farm's layout. Its name pill shows the lock, as today. When it opens, the lot clears and its recipe stands. This: - tells the farm's story (it starts overgrown and you bring it back); - lets wave 1 ship with only the places chapter 1 opens; - costs four frames in place of twelve locked buildings.

KF9: the style is kits.md §5's, with three farm notes: - warm and sunny: greens and straw golds, as the Hub's farmlands page is today; - every building readable at a glance: each place's wall set, roof colour and landmark together make it unique on its row; - no text and no Uma art: even the notice board's papers and the Shop's sign are blank shapes.

KF10: the seasons are the stage's light, not the kit's tiles. The four seasons (game mechanics) change the light and a colour grade: a fresh spring, a bright summer, an amber autumn, and a cold, pale winter. The engine does that live, so the kit is drawn once in a neutral late-spring light. Snow on the roofs and bare trees in winter are a later wave, if the light alone doesn't sell it.

5. What isn't in this kit (KF11)

KF11: the kit is the farm's ground and buildings only. These come from elsewhere: - Umas: sprite sets from uma-platform's sprite pipeline, and standees for outfits without one. - Animals (the Coop's and the Barn's): the ranch's work adds them to the stage, with a kit minor or a sprite request then (the roadmap). - Items: uma-art's items and the UI marks, drawn by uma-kit over the stage. - The minigames' scenes: the Kitchen's counter, the Restaurant's floor and the Forge's board are their own kits, made with each workshop's game (the roadmap). - The romance's event backdrops: heart events keep their full-screen sheet (game mechanics) until the stage plays events (later work).

6. What the kit must hold (KF12)

KF12: kitMissing for kind farm (uma-stage 0.2.1's KIT_REQUIRED.farm, and FARM_WAVE_2 for wave 2) checks wave 1's frames by name: - Roles: floor*, riser*, building-wall*, window*, door*, roof*. Each of the plank and plaster sets has 2 walls, 2 windows and a door, and each of the four roof sets has 2 roofs. - Floors: - grass-1–4, path-1–2, tilled-1–2, forest-floor-1–2, water-1–2, paving-1–2 (14); - risers: riser-grass-1–2 (a grassy lip) and riser-bank (wet stones over water) (3). - Buildings (20): - building-wall-plank-1–2, window-plank-1–2, door-plank, door-wide-plank; - building-wall-plaster-1–2, window-plaster-1–2, door-plaster, door-shop; - roof-red-1–2, roof-thatch-1–2, roof-shingle-1–2, roof-slate-1–2. - Props (35): - the forest: tree-1–3, stump, mushroom-patch, herb-patch; - the Fields and the Fishery: scarecrow, dock, reeds; - the stations: mill-sails, chimney, smoker, log-pile, saw-bench; - the yard and the town: bin, crates, barrel, sack, gate-arch, fence-1, fence-2, fence-broken, stall-awning; - the homestead: porch, flower-box, lot-stakes; - overgrown lots and decorations: weeds-1, weeds-2, brambles, flowers-1, flowers-2, bush-1, bush-2, rock-small, lantern. - Crops (15): crop-sprout, crop-growing, crop-withered, and the twelve ripe families of KF6.

Wave 1 is 87 frames. Wave 2 adds 30, and kitMissing checks them once the farm draws a wave-2 place on the stage: - floors and walls (11): - the stone set: building-wall-stone-1–2, window-stone-1–2, door-stone; - the Mine's cliff: wall-rock and wall-top-rock; - the Quarry's pit: quarry-1–2 and riser-quarry; - the ranch's ground: paddock; - props (19): mine-mouth, ore-cart, quarry-rock-1–2, stone-stack, furnace, grindstone, anvil, forge-chimney, ring-bench, trough, hay-bale, awning, cafe-table, herb-box, bunting, flagpole, signpost, notice-board.

Both waves fit in two 2048 pages (about 3.7 million texels of 8.4 million).

7. Delivery (KF13, KF14)

KF13: delivered as kits.md §8 says, one uma-art minor a wave (F9): 1. Draw the wave's frames to §2–§4, in KF9's style. 2. Pack them with the export script into atlas.png (and atlas-1.png) and atlas.json. Commit the masters and the script, so the export is the same byte for byte each run. 3. Make the review sheets: - the kit on light and dark; - a sample band (the Fields, the Mill and the Coop as an overgrown lot, with the path row under them), built by a stand-in render or uma-stage's builder; - a scale sheet with the stand-in traveler beside a door, a tree and the bin. 4. Release the uma-art minor. The owner approves the look (the gate). The first farm change that draws the wave pins the release and swaps out the stand-in's frames.

KF14: the names are the farm's API. The stage's stand-in kit draws every name in flat colours: soil brown, plaster cream, red roofs. So the art swaps in with no code change, and a missing frame falls back to its stand-in rather than breaking the farm.

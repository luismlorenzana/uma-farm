# Four-season crew balance
F117, November 24, 2026. Each case ran 120 consecutive in-game days with the ordinary morning shift and end-of-day rules. Test farms began at Rank 10 to give every roster access to the same jobs. The results measure farm work under sustained demand.

## How much land the crew can handle

| Farmhands | On shift | 40 tiles per plot | 24 tiles per plot | 16 tiles per plot |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | 8–9 | 1 plot · 40 planned tiles | 1 plot · 24 planned tiles | 2 plots · 32 planned tiles |
| 30 | 12–13 | 2 plots · 80 planned tiles | 3 plots · 72 planned tiles | 5 plots · 80 planned tiles |
| 50 | 21–22 | 4 plots · 160 planned tiles | 7 plots · 168 planned tiles | 8 plots · 128 planned tiles |

A balanced result fed every animal, collected every available product the same shift, brushed at least 85% of animal-days, watered at least 99% of crops that could still ripen, harvested at least 95% of ripe crop opportunities and started at least 90% of ready station orders on the day they were available. Each of the four gathering activities had to run in every season. The worst season had to average at least 80% of planned tiles occupied by crops across days 7–22.

The farm had 12 animals split evenly between Coop and Barn, five recurring station targets at level 2, a Kitchen and access to every gathering area. Each station began with 360 exact-grade inputs set aside from shipping. A recurring demand removed up to eight units of its output every four days. The player bought seasonal seed and fodder. The crew had staggered launch professions, an aptitude head start and tier 3 watering can, hoe and brush. No player actions were used on crops, animals, gathering, cooking, stations or shipping. Each active plot began with a row of weeds and a row of clear soil, with any other planned rows tilled. Crops were carrot, tomato, wheat and oats in season order. The capacity figures apply to this annual rotation. Orchards use the same field jobs, and established trees skip routine watering.

## Changes in this pass
- Feed and collect come early. On crowded farms the crew collects from both homes before preparing more field space.
- Ready planned stations get their first load ahead of field expansion. Routine cooking and surplus shipping preserve a refill batch of the exact ingredients.
- With row watering available, newly sown tiles can wait for the rest of their row, then get watered in one pass. Established dry crops still get priority. The crew keeps time for watering before the shift runs out.
- Near a seasonal boundary, Auto Work spends water where a crop can still ripen or keep growing across the boundary. Orchards and the marked festival crop stay eligible.

The short answer

  • One engine, shared, not copied (FR1, FR2). uma-quest's HD-2D stage (three.js, a tile-built world, pixel sprites, lights and fog) becomes a shared uma-platform package once it has been measured on the phone. The farm is its second user, so the platform extracts it (P11). The farm never imports uma-quest, and it doesn't copy an engine that's still changing.
  • Home becomes a walkable farm, still portrait (FR3, FR5): five rows of three for the lines, a workshop row, the Warehouse and the Post, and a Gate the Umas come in by; about 9 tiles across, so each place gets 3 tiles of width, and the farm scrolls up and down.
  • Lots of taps (FR9). Tap a place, tap a slot, pick an Uma. She walks in through the Gate to her slot. Tap the bubble over her when the work is done, and the items fly to the Warehouse. Tap Repeat, or call her home and she runs back out.
  • Umas are uma-quest's sprites (FR10–FR13) with their walks and runs reused whole: 8 outfits today, and at most 170 of the 272 ever. The rest stand as standees, her face on an upright card, and become sprites when their set is published. Every outfit still works (FM6). Farm-work poses are a request for later (FR11).
  • The buildings and plots are a farm kit (FR15, FR16) in uma-quest's kit format, drawn by ChatGPT. "3D" means the HD-2D look: tiles given height, and modular fronts and upright cards lit in 3D. There are no 3D models.
  • The loop ends in workshops (FR17–FR22), each a building with its own minigame, each an original game in a popular mobile genre: the Kitchen, a cooking game; the Restaurant, a serving game; the Forge, whose genre is your pick (merge is the recommendation). They turn made items into the games' items, inputs are never lost, and better play makes more (FM9 stands).
  • What stays: the rules (F4) and the save and sync (F3); the cloud; parcels, the accept lists and the Post (FS22–FS31, uma-platform's PO1).

What it changes once answered: plan.md: FM18 (portrait on uma-kit's shell), FM19 (Home on uma-kit's Hub map) and FM9 (the Forge) · first-playable.md: FW6, and the M4 tasks F8 and F9 · spec.md: §4 (FS12–FS21, the smithing game) and FS32 (the queue). Until then nothing is built, and the farm keeps playing on today's map.

The farm, drawn (FR5–FR8)

FR5's layout through uma-quest's camera: orthographic and pitched 35° down, so a tile's depth shows at sin 35° ≈ 0.57 of its width. Invented shapes stand in for the farm kit (FR15): plots are floor tiles, ore, crops and trees are upright cards, buildings are fronts and roofs. A capsule and a round head is a sprite; a card on a base with a plain face circle is a standee (FR12). Dashed lines are walks from the Gate.

FR5 · the layout, 9 tiles wide

  • Each place is 3 tiles wide and about 3 deep, with its four slots marked on its plot or in front of its building.
  • Rows are the lines, gather → process → make from left to right, as on today's map (FM19's order).
  • The workshop row sits below the lines and holds the end of the loop. Workshops have no slots: you play them (FR17).
  • The Gate is where the Umas enter and leave. The Warehouse and the Post flank it at the bottom, where the thumb is.

FR6 · the camera

uma-quest's: orthographic, pitched 35°, pixel-snapped. It pans only up and down; a flick glides it and it settles on a row. No zoom, since one texel to one pixel allows no free scaling, and no sideways pan, since the farm is exactly the screen's width.

FR7 · the frame stays uma-kit's

The account bar, the bottom bar (Farm · Warehouse · Menu) and the sync warning sit over the stage as they do today. The Ready badges and "Collect all" stay.

FR8 · a place's page is a sheet over the farm

It holds uma-kit's work screens as they are (slot rows, the job sheet, queues, Recall, Repeat, quick looks), so F5's screens carry over. The farm stays visible above the sheet, so you watch her walk off when you assign her.

Drawn here, not settled by the doc (for F11 to decide):

  • Lanes. Three places × 3 tiles fill all 9, so no tile is left for paths up between the columns. They're drawn as narrow lanes on the column edges, which takes a little width from the places.
  • Heights. Fronts are drawn one level tall. A true storey is 256 texels (kits.md), which at 35° hides about 3.5 tiles of ground behind it, more than a 3-deep row: the slots of the row above would be hidden unless fronts stay low or slots stand in front.
  • One screen. At true scale the whole farm is about one phone screen tall (below), so the up-and-down pan matters most while a sheet is open.

On the phone, ground to scale (FR3, FR6–FR8)

FR3's numbers: a tile is 128 texels and one texel is one device pixel, so an iPhone in portrait (1,179 px wide) shows about 9.2 tiles across. Seen at 35°, a tile of ground is 128 × sin 35° ≈ 73 px deep. Figures and fronts are drawn smaller than the engine's true size (a sprite's cell is 256 texels, two tiles).

FR10 · who walks and how: uma-quest's field animations, reused whole

Arriving · at work · done

  • Arriving: she enters at the Gate and walks the path to her slot with walk-front, walk-back, walk-left, walk-right at 6 frames × 110 ms.
  • At work: she stands on her turnaround still, facing the place's work, with a nod every few seconds.
  • Done: she plays wave and a Ready bubble rises over her.

Leaving

  • She runs back out with run-* at 6 frames × 75 ms: after Recall, after Collect without Repeat, or when a queue stops.
  • Paths follow the farm's path tiles; she walks at uma-quest's field speed, so crossing the farm takes a few seconds.

FR14 · the counts stay right

  • Sprites only show the save; they never change it.
  • Where an Uma walks is drawn from her work row (uma-work's entries and timers), so the scene can't drift from the rules.
  • A save that loads mid-job places her at her slot at once, without the walk.

One idea, short of a call: a quick assign. Long-press an Uma's face in the album and tap a place, so she walks to its first free slot. It's left for your review.

Sprites and standees (FR11–FR13)

Who has a sprite set (FR12), drawn to scale over the 272 outfits

Today 8 of the 272 outfits have a set (5 travelers and 3 townsfolk; P108). The plans stop at 100 travelers and 70 townsfolk, so about 102 outfits never get one. Every outfit still works (FM6).

A standee, and how it becomes a walker

  • Her face (master data's outfit-faces, which the album already shows) on an upright card on a small base; drawn here as a plain circle.
  • Lit and shadowed like a sprite; it glides along the path with a small bob, with no walk cycle.
  • client.spriteSet returns a set or null, so each batch uma-platform publishes turns more standees into walkers with no farm change.

Memory and battery (FR13)

  • The problem: a set is about 1.6 MB to download and about 14 MB of GPU memory while on screen (uma-quest D16). Sixty distinct sets would be about 840 MB, far too much.
  • The rule: only Umas in view are drawn as sprites, at most 8 live sets (about 112 MB), nearest the camera's centre first. The rest stay standees until a set frees up.
  • Downloads: on first sight, cached on the phone by their hash (Workbox's cache-first rule for /api/master/files/), so each downloads once.
  • Battery: saver on by default (30 fps, no blur or glow), as in uma-quest (DN27). The farm records its own frame rate and battery through the shared recorder before the cap is raised.

The farm kit (FR15–FR16)

FR15: built in uma-quest's kit format (its docs/design/kits.md). There are no 3D models: the engine builds height from tiles and stands everything else up as lit cards. That's the HD-2D look. Below, a place built from kit pieces, in the same invented shapes.

The pieces

  • Plots are floor variants: tilled soil, a quarry floor, a forest floor, a pond edge.
  • Ore, crops, trees, the pond and the stalls are prop cards, and each place has three stages (empty, working, ready).
  • Buildings are modular fronts (building-wall, window, roof, door, sign) for the workbenches, the making places, the workshops, the Warehouse and the Post.
  • Paths and fences are floor and wall-top pieces, and the Gate is a prop.

Three openings the format needs (part of FR1's request)

  1. A kit kind beyond biome and town: farm.
  2. Farm markers: place, slot, gate, path.
  3. Town kits' KIT_REQUIRED signs made per kind.

FR18 · the Kitchen's cooking game

  • A counter of burners and a pass; orders come in as tickets (a Party Meal needs its three parts cooked).
  • You drop each ingredient on a burner, and its ring fills; you pull it in the green and plate it before the ticket's timer runs out.
  • Burnt or late parts cost points, never inputs. The difficulty is juggling several burners at once.
  • A play lasts about 60 seconds, and an Uma from the crew is the cook: she runs between the burners and the pass (FR10's run-*).

FR19 · the Restaurant's serving game, and what it's for

Guests arrive in waves. You seat them, take each order, bring the dish from the hatch and clear the table, and tips go up with speed. The server is an Uma running between tables. About 60 seconds. What it serves is the farm's own:

  • Recommended the Restaurant serves the crew: a good shift gives your Umas a "well fed" boost, working faster (×1.25 for a few hours) and growing their Craft Level faster. An economy loop with no currency (FS8).
  • Alternative: guests pay tips that become a farm currency spent on boosts. It needs a currency, which FS8 rejected.

FR21 · one contract for every minigame

  • A play is 30–90 seconds, on a seed the game shows, so a board can be learnt and luck never decides the result alone.
  • It scores 0–100: Rough below 50 (the base yield), Good 50–74 (×1.25), Fine 75–89 (×1.5), Masterwork 90+ (×2).
  • Inputs are never lost: the yield never falls below the base, and an unfinished play counts as Rough with no retries (FS17).
  • A play costs its inputs only, and there's a practice mode with no inputs and no yield. The sketches below are that practice mode.
  • It runs one thumb in portrait, with timing from pointerdown.
  • FR22 · rules first, scenes after. Each minigame's rules (its seeds, scoring and tiers) are pure code in @farm/domain with tests before any scene draws them; the scene plays the events back. It needs no engine, so it can start before the stage package exists (FR2). The sketches keep that split: plain rules, and a page drawing them.

The rules as built

  • Three burners and a pass. Tickets arrive on the seed shown, every 2.4–3.8 s; the last comes 12 s before the end. A Party Meal needs Roast, Stew and Bread and waits 18 s; a Tonic needs Herbs and Broth and waits 13 s (about half the tickets are meals).
  • Tap an empty burner: it starts the next part of the first ticket. Tap a ticket to put it first.
  • The ring fills (Herbs 1.8 s, Bread 2.0, Broth 2.4, Stew 2.6, Roast 3.2) into a 1-second green window. Tap the burner in the green to pull the part to the pass: the middle 0.3 s is Perfect +4, the rest of the green Good +2. Too early: raw −1, cook it again. Past the green: burnt −3, and the pan smokes 0.7 s.
  • Served: when a ticket's parts are all on the pass, +3 a part (a meal +9, a tonic +6). Late: a ticket whose timer runs out leaves, −4.
  • Score = points ÷ the seed's most (7 a part: Perfect + served) × 100, held to 0–100. ✕ or leaving the page stops the play, and it counts as Rough.

The rules as built

  • Four tables, a hatch and a door. Guests come in waves on the seed shown: 2–3 groups a wave, a wave every 11–14 s; the last wave comes 16 s before the end.
  • Seat: tap a group at the door, then a free table. They read the menu for 2 s, then show Order!
  • Take the order: tap the table. The waiter runs there (about a floor's width a second) and the kitchen cooks it in 3.5 s. Bring it: tap the hatch, and he picks up every ready dish; tap the table to serve. They eat for 4 s and leave a tip. Clear: tap the table; he takes the tip and frees it.
  • Mood: each group's bar drains while they wait on you (at the door, with an order up, or with their dish waiting): 13 s from full to a walkout, −4. Each step done for them gives back 15%.
  • Tips go up with speed: 4 to 10 by mood as they leave (the full 10 at 80% or more). Score = tips ÷ (10 a group) × 100, held to 0–100. At the end, tips still on tables count and groups still eating pay; the rest score nothing. ✕ or leaving the page counts as Rough.
  • Well fed (FR19, the sketch's reading): your crew works ×1.25 faster for 2 hours × the tier's multiplier: Rough 2 h, Good 2 h 30, Fine 3 h, Masterwork 4 h.
